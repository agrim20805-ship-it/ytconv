# What is copyright, and how do Creative Commons licences change it?

## Short answer
Copyright protects what people make - documents, images, videos and sound recordings - so that others can't use it unfairly [University of South Australia 00:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=5s), and it applies automatically as soon as something is created [Matt P. Wolf 00:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=29s). The owner has exclusive rights to reproduce, publish, perform and adapt the work, so using it that way without permission infringes copyright [University of South Australia 00:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=26s) [University of South Australia 00:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=43s). Creative Commons, a nonprofit founded in 2001 [Wikimedia UK 02:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=124s), gives creators free licences that grant permission in advance [Matt P. Wolf 00:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=58s) [Matt P. Wolf 01:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=63s). Every Creative Commons licence lets people share the work as long as they credit the creator [Matt P. Wolf 02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=163s), and unlike plain copyright it lets others reuse, copy and modify the work without asking [Wikimedia UK 02:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=154s).

## What copyright gives an owner
- Owners have exclusive rights to control how others use their work, including the right to reproduce it in any format, publish it, perform or communicate it, and adapt it [University of South Australia 00:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=26s) [University of South Australia 00:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=30s).
- Using someone's work, or a substantial part of it, in these ways without permission is infringing copyright [University of South Australia 00:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=43s).
- Owners also have moral rights: using someone's work in an assignment without acknowledging them is plagiarism, even if the work has already been shared online [University of South Australia 00:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=51s) [University of South Australia 00:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=56s).
- If you take a photo on your phone, you own it and have the right to exploit it for your own commercial benefit [Wikimedia UK 00:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=21s).
- You must get someone's permission before sharing or reusing their work, even when it is posted online [Matt P. Wolf 00:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=42s).

## Where copyright came from
- Britain has had copyright laws since the 1600s, when printing companies wanted to stop other printers selling copies of their books [Wikimedia UK 00:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=28s).
- The 1709 Statute of Anne, often seen as the first real copyright law, gave publishers rights for a fixed period: 14 years, renewable for a similar term [Wikimedia UK 00:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=52s) [Wikimedia UK 01:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=63s).
- The US Constitution took the same 14-year term, renewable once, in 1787 [Wikimedia UK 01:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=69s).
- The 1886 Berne Convention said authors do not have to assert copyright for it to apply, and extended the term to 50 years after the author's death [Wikimedia UK 01:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=79s).
- Today, in most of the world, copyright lasts for the author's life plus 50 or 70 years; it usually ends with the calendar year, so January 1st is celebrated as Public Domain Day [Wikimedia UK 01:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=101s) [Wikimedia UK 01:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=107s).
- The video dates the WTO's TRIPS agreement to 1985 [Wikimedia UK 01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=90s); the agreement was signed in 1994 (not from the videos).

## What a student may copy
- Students may copy a reasonable portion of someone else's work for research and study [University of South Australia 01:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=71s).
- As a general rule that means up to 10% of a book, even online, a whole article, or an image - as long as you acknowledge your sources [University of South Australia 01:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=82s) [University of South Australia 01:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=87s).
- Using someone's work without permission outside your studies may be breaking the law [University of South Australia 01:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=104s).
- Sharing lecture notes, readings, past exam papers or journal articles on unauthorised sharing sites breaches copyright and the student code of conduct [University of South Australia 02:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=136s).

## How Creative Commons licences work
- A licence refines your copyright and streamlines how you give permission [Matt P. Wolf 01:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=63s).
- Licences are made of four rules, each with its own symbol: attribution (credit the creator) [Matt P. Wolf 01:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=107s), non-commercial (no one else may make money from the work) [Matt P. Wolf 01:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=117s), no derivatives (no changes without asking) [Matt P. Wolf 02:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=132s), and share alike (new works must carry the same licence) [Matt P. Wolf 02:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=148s).
- Attribution is in every licence; the other three are optional, which makes six combinations [Matt P. Wolf 02:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=177s) [Matt P. Wolf 03:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=183s).
- The Attribution licence gives re-users the most freedom and Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives the least [Matt P. Wolf 03:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=191s).
- Like normal copyright, a Creative Commons licence keeps the author's right to be identified as the creator [Wikimedia UK 02:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=143s). Wikimedia's own content is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 [Wikimedia UK 02:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=133s).
- Creative Commons work can be found with the search filter on the Creative Commons website, Jamendo for music and Flickr for images [Matt P. Wolf 04:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=249s) [Matt P. Wolf 04:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=257s).

- By default you need the owner's permission to reuse a work [University of South Australia 00:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=43s) [Matt P. Wolf 00:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=42s).
- Acknowledging the creator matters, under copyright and under Creative Commons [University of South Australia 00:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=56s) [Wikimedia UK 02:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=143s) [Matt P. Wolf 02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=163s).

## Where they differ
- **Permission.** Matt P. Wolf says you must always get permission before sharing or reusing someone's work [Matt P. Wolf 00:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBsoNSCmrno&t=42s), while the University of South Australia says students may copy a reasonable portion for research and study [University of South Australia 01:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=71s). They are talking about different situations: the university video is about study, the Creative Commons video about sharing in general.
- **What copyright is for.** The University of South Australia presents copyright as something that protects everyone, including you [University of South Australia 02:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=151s), and asks students to comply with it [University of South Australia 02:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psUEVRtYzpA&t=161s). Wikimedia UK presents open licences as a response to how widespread copyright has become [Wikimedia UK 01:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=117s) and wants more content shared freely [Wikimedia UK 03:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=203s).
- **Social media.** Only Wikimedia UK says that uploading to Facebook, Twitter or Instagram gives those companies the right to reuse your content without asking or crediting you [Wikimedia UK 03:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4CcSfdC-PQ&t=180s).
